# Ideas (not in scope yet)

Anything outside the current slice goes here, one line each. Picked up only after the freeze is lifted or a slice is added by /build-plan.

- Bake-off: retest gpt-oss-120b with a larger `maxTokens` on the tiny smoke call; its reply was cut off at 50 tokens (`{"`), so the day-0 result is not a fair test.
- Slice 8 gotcha (found in slice 5): `queue_over_capacity` and `exception_queue_processed` link to every held request, so following *all* `caused_by` links from one student's eligibility also reaches other students' events. The detector should follow the student's own path (as `path()` in tests/test_chain.py does), not every ancestor.
- Hero hit rate (found in slice 6b, 0 of 9 real runs): a disruption lasts one class, so students lose 1–2 of 16 classes and stay far above 80%. A `duration_weeks` (or sessions) field on a disruption in the blueprint would let a strike or storm span several classes. Changes the Blueprint schema (slice 3a) and the term loop (slice 5): needs a team decision.
- Partial acceptance of exception requests: today a request covering one recorded absence and one future class is refused whole (run L-b0631988, slice 6c). Accepting the recorded sessions would be an engine change (slice 4), and it would change what the stress test finds: team decision.
- Level 3 (after Demo Day or as a roadmap line in the pitch): the AI drafts a new template from a policy no template covers, a teammate checks and saves it, and it joins the library; later, with the full 4-stage validation, the AI's template is used directly.
- More templates on the same building blocks (each about 1–2 h): late assignment submission, fee or scholarship document deadlines, library fines, staff leave.
- Full exploration loop (nice-to-have 11): random variants alongside targeted ones, outcome-based prioritisation, follow-up rounds on variants that broke.
- Agents often ask for an exception before the class has happened (refused, correctly). Worth showing in the UI as a real finding about the policy's wording ("requests only after the absence is recorded").
- Blueprint fill retried once in 10 real runs on 24 Nov (41.5 s, over the 40 s target; the other 9 took 21-28 s). The reason a reply is retried is now recorded in `retried` and printed by `blueprint create`: check it when it happens again, and tighten the fill prompt for that rule.
- Fresh AI blueprints can be calm: on 24 Nov, B-7013e667 had one transport obstacle on one session hitting 2 students, and its real run (L-7c391cbb) made only 4 decisions; the converted 6b scenario (two obstacles, one hitting all 4 students) made 14 and 11. Old 6b blueprints with one disruption also gave 6-7. Worth a fill-prompt nudge ("disruptions that hit several people across the term") or leaving it to the stress variants (L8): team decision.
- A reply whose `reason` runs past 300 characters fails validation twice and becomes an error no-action; in a 9-decision run one such error is 11%, so the run ends `degraded` (L-fb337e81, faculty, 24 Nov). **Done 24 Nov:** an over-long reason or strategy now keeps its whole sentences that fit, marked "…", instead of failing the reply (a model-written summary was weighed and dropped: one more call per long reply, inside the 40 s phase limit, and it can fail too).
- The variants prompt's misses figure assumes no cancelled occurrences; a variant that cancels some (L8's "Late Checkpoint with Session Cancellations") makes each miss count more than the figure says. Recomputing it per variant would be about 15 minutes in `explore.misses_to_fail` (24 Nov).
- A consenting person's context lists every pending request, not only those awaiting their consent; the curfew guardian's prompt (L9c) would be clearer with just theirs. About 10 minutes in `Term.context` (24 Nov, L9a). **Done 24 Nov (L9d):** the prompt lists the requests waiting for their consent.
- A weekly capacity shared across nightly slots (the curfew plan said the warden decides within a weekly capacity; L9c uses a capacity per night, since a queue processed each step has its capacity per slot). About 30 minutes in `Engine._queue_slot` plus a template field (24 Nov, L9c).
- An LLM-decided queue processed each step logs `decision_awaiting` and `queue_processed` every slot, even with nothing offered: 80 extra events in a curfew term. Skipping empty slots would keep the timeline readable (about 10 minutes in `open_decisions`/`close_decisions`, 24 Nov, L9c).
- A refused consent is not on the failed check's chain: the late night's record links to the check directly, so the chain doesn't show "the guardian said no, they came back late anyway". Linking a record to a rejected request covering it would add that step (slice 8 or 9 decides, 24 Nov, L9c). Slice 8 left it out: the chain follows recorded links only, so slice 9 decides. The same gap shows on curfew bottlenecks: a late night covered by a request filed in advance links to the check through the request, so the late night itself is not on the chain.
- The curfew fill takes 40–44 s (8 profiles are 43% of its ~1,300 output tokens), so a validation retry no longer fits the 55 s budget and would end `invalid`. Accepted at first (team, 24 Nov); if a curfew fill ever ends invalid, ask for shorter profiles in the shared fill prompt (1–2 goals, at most 2 constraints): about 10 minutes plus 3 real re-runs. **Done 24 Nov:** two changes, measured on Bedrock. Profiles capped at 2 goals and 2 constraints (curfew 38.1–40.1 s, a small gain), then every model call asked for compact single-line JSON: the model had been pretty-printing, 40% of a fill's characters were whitespace. Curfew fills now take 28.9–30.0 s (889–908 output tokens), attendance 24.7 s (741); agent replies fell from about 150 to about 97 output tokens. A fill's retry still only just fits: after a 30 s first try about 25 s remain, and a fill takes about 27 s. Shorter profiles kept (team, 24 Nov): they save about 2 s for almost no loss, since the model was already writing about 2 goals and 1–2 constraints.
- In run L-692db8f4, residents hit by a two-night delay thought their night-12 request also covered night 13, and didn't ask again; on night 14 they said it was too late. Their prompt shows pending requests but not which nights their decided requests covered. Listing them ("R1: night 12, approved") would stop the mix-up; about 15 minutes in prompts.py (24 Nov, L9d). **Done 24 Nov:** every person who files requests now sees each one with the steps it covers and what became of it ("R1 (night 12): approved"). Re-run L-fa98e6a9 on the same blueprint: on night 13, 2 of the 4 delayed residents asked again and were approved (0 of 4 before); the researcher still wrote that its night-12 request covered tonight. A further step, if it matters for the demo: say "tonight is night 13" next to the obstacle in the prompt (about 10 minutes). **Done 24 Nov:** every prompt now opens with "Now: night 13 (week 3)" and names the step on each event line; checked on Bedrock in run L-8506e5a9: all 4 delayed residents asked for night 12 and again for night 13, the guardian consented and the warden approved all 4 each night before it was recorded, and on night 14 each named the request that covered night 13.
- `test_fill_gets_what_is_left_of_the_shared_budget` fails when tests/test_blueprint.py runs alone (54.375 against 54.5 ± 0.1): the first fill also builds the values model, about 0.1 s, which earlier tests have already cached in a full run. It failed the same way before 24 Nov's changes. Widening its tolerance, or warming the cache in the test, is a 2-minute fix (24 Nov). Seen once in a full run too (54.28, 24 Nov evening, during 7a); it passed on both reruns. **Done 24 Nov:** the test now checks the fill's budget is at most 55 − 0.5 s and at least 53.5 s, instead of 54.5 ± 0.1.
- A ladder check records `check_evaluated` only when a person reaches a new step, so in a curfew run where nobody reaches the warning there is no check event, and a late-entry request still waiting then can't show up as "pending at the check" (the 7a run L-d50a0a92: 0 check events, 0 findings). Recording a weekly check for anyone with pending requests would let the detector see that bottleneck; about 20 minutes in `run_checks` plus a test (24 Nov, slice 8). **Done 24 Nov:** a ladder period with no new step records where the person stands (`standing`, no `outcome`, so it notifies nobody) when a request for a night already recorded is still waiting; a fixed curfew test gives "Late-entry backlog leaves residents facing curfew penalties" for the researcher in week 1, at 1 late return. Not yet seen in a real curfew run.
- In the hero run L-ff6f0ca8, faculty "corrected" the working student's record to absent when it already said absent (E-0053, E-0092), and those no-change corrections sit on the hero chain. Refusing a correction that changes nothing (a precondition in the template's `correct_record` effect), or leaving no-change corrections off the chain, would make the evidence read cleaner; about 15 minutes plus a test (25 Nov, slice 10).
- `explore hunt` always starts again from the first approved variant, so re-hunting a parent re-runs variants that already came back empty. A `--skip <blueprint_id>` option, or skipping variants that already have a completed run with 0 findings, would save about 25–45 calls a re-hunt (25 Nov, slice 10).
- Each deploy keeps its release in `/opt/rulestorm/releases/<tag>` and never removes old ones (about 100 MB each with the venv). Keeping only the last 3 in `deploy.sh` is a 5-minute change (25 Nov, slice 11).
- The development PC runs Python 3.12 and the server 3.14; all 189 tests passed on both on 25 Nov. A teammate PC on 3.14, or a 3.14 test run before each deploy tag, would catch a version-only break early (25 Nov, slice 11).
- Port 80 on the server is open to everyone so `http://15.134.48.1` stays as the backup link. Allowing only CloudFront (the AWS-managed prefix list `com.amazonaws.global.cloudfront.origin-facing` in `rulestorm-web`) would stop people skipping CloudFront, but it drops that backup; about 5 minutes, best done just before the freeze (25 Nov, slice 12).
- The status-check alarm sees the server, not the app: if `rulestorm.service` stops while the server is fine, nobody is emailed. A 5-minute cron on the server that curls `/api/health` and publishes to `rulestorm-alerts` on failure would cover it inside the free allowances; about 20 minutes plus a policy on the role (25 Nov, slice 12).
